# 2.4.2 · Guards and the guest path

**Type:** access control · **API calls: 0**
**Depends on:** 2.4.1

RLS is the boundary (2.4.1). This phase is about the layer above it doing its
job *well* — which mostly means telling people the truth when it turns them
away.

## The gap this found

`requireTeacher()` redirects a non-teacher to `/?error=forbidden`. **Nothing
rendered that.** A student who followed a teacher link — or a bookmark, or a
shared URL — landed on the home page with no explanation, which reads as a
broken link rather than a page that is not theirs.

The home page now says so, and points at what they *can* do:

> Trang quản lý tài liệu chỉ dành cho giáo viên. Bạn vẫn có thể đặt câu hỏi về
> tuyển sinh ở ngay bên dưới.

A rejection that does not explain itself gets reported as a bug. This one costs
three lines.

## The guest surface, measured

```
GET  /                     200   chat, no account needed
GET  /login                200
GET  /api/health           200
GET  /history              307 → /login
GET  /teacher/documents    307 → /login
GET  /api/documents        401
POST /api/documents        401
DELETE /api/documents/:id  401
POST /api/chat             400   reached and validated -- open by design
```

`/api/chat` is deliberately the one open endpoint, which is why it carries its
own rate limit (2.3.1).

**401 versus 403 stays meaningful:** 401 means "log in", 403 means logging in
will not help. Collapsing them would send a student to a login page that cannot
solve their problem.
